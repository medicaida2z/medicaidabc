Frequently Asked Questions

A. How to appeal a Pennsylvania Medicaid decision

If your Long-Term Care Medicaid application is denied, delayed, or terminated, file a written appeal using the appeal section on your PA DHS notice with your local County Assistance Office (CAO) within 30 calendar days of the mailing date on the notice to preserve your right to a Bureau of Hearings and Appeals (BHA) fair hearing.

B. How to apply for PA Long-Term Care Medicaid

Applications can be submitted online via COMPASS PA (compass.state.pa.us), by mail, or in person at your county CAO. You must complete both financial verification (PA 600L) and the clinical level-of-care evaluation (Nursing Facility Clinically Eligible / NFCE assessment) to establish eligibility.

C. How to request required documents from banks and businesses

When preparing for the mandatory 5-year (60-month) look-back audit, request complete historical monthly statements directly from bank branch managers or institutional records departments. For closed bank accounts, life insurance policies, or annuities, submit formal written requests for "cash surrender value verification letters" and 60-month historical transaction ledgers.

D. Denial Information & Important Deadlines

If PA DHS issues a Notice of Denial or Termination (Form PA 162), check the specific reason code (such as missing verification documents, excess resources, or uncompensated transfer penalty) and track these strict deadlines immediately:

  • 30-Day Appeal Deadline: You have 30 calendar days from the mailing date on the PA 162 notice to submit a written appeal request to the County Assistance Office.
  • 10-Day / 15-Day Benefit Continuation Deadline: If an existing Medicaid benefit is being terminated or reduced, you must appeal within 10 days (or before the effective date listed on the notice, typically 15 days) to keep benefits active while the appeal is pending.
  • Verification Cure / Reconsideration Window: If denied solely for missing documents, submitting the missing records promptly (often within 30 to 60 days depending on CAO case status) may allow reopening without starting a brand-new application.
  • 3-Month Retroactive Coverage Rule: PA Medicaid can cover unpaid nursing facility bills up to 3 calendar months prior to the month of application—missing a reapplication deadline can permanently forfeit retroactive coverage for earlier months.

E. Representative Payee & Managing Benefit Checks

A Representative Payee is an individual or organization appointed by the Social Security Administration (SSA) or VA to receive and manage monthly benefit checks for someone who cannot manage their own finances. Under Pennsylvania Long-Term Care Medicaid rules, the Representative Payee must ensure the resident's monthly income (minus the personal needs allowance and any spousal diversion) is paid directly toward their nursing facility patient-pay liability and keep strict accounting records for PA DHS audits.
